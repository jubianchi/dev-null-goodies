> Everything you write to /dev/null disappears. This one sticks.

Every Unix machine has a black hole. Now you can wear one.

This iron-on transfer is based on the logo of [/dev/null](https://dev-null.sh), the social network for developers.

## Why you need one

- Merch you compile yourself. No shipping, no sold-out sizes: any t-shirt in your drawer becomes swag.
- `| tee`: pipe /dev/null onto any shirt. Dark or light, there's a version for it.
- Give a second life to that plain tee, or to the hackathon shirt nobody remembers.

## What's in the 3MF

- 2 transfers, one per plate: dark (for light fabrics) or light (for dark fabrics)
- Plates ready to slice, colors already assigned

## How to iron it on

1. Before removing the print from the plate, cover it with transfer or painter's tape to keep the pieces aligned.
2. Place the t-shirt flat and position the logo, clean side facing up.
3. Hold it with heat-resistant tape (e.g. Kapton) and remove the transfer or painter's tape.
4. Cover with baking/parchment paper.
5. Press with a hot iron (no steam) for 15–20 seconds, moving evenly for larger designs.
6. Let it cool slightly and remove the paper and the tape.

## Print profile

Not part of the description: these go in the print profile form.

**Name:** 0.08mm, 2 layers, tuned purge

**Description:**

- Bambu PLA Basic: black, red, white
- 0.08 mm layer height, 2 layers (0.16 mm in total), no supports
- Top surface ironed (flow 8%, spacing 0.15 mm, 30 mm/s)
- Higher purge volumes when switching to white (750 from black, 650 from red): the visible side is the first layer, so
  any leftover color would show on the fabric
